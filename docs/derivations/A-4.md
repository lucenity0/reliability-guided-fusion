# A-4: What routing weights mean under L, P and F

Issue: #15 (internal).
Specification: [MATH_SPEC §4](../MATH_SPEC.md).
These are algebraic interpretations conditional on a fixed input and fixed
expert outputs, not causal explanations of the input modalities.

Let $\pi=\operatorname{softmax}(g)$, $p_k=\sigma(z_k)$, and
$q_k(y)=p_k^y(1-p_k)^{1-y}$ for $y\in\{0,1\}$. All logits are finite.

## L: logit mixture

$$
z_L=\sum_k\pi_kz_k,\qquad p_L=\sigma(z_L),\qquad
\frac{p_L}{1-p_L}=\prod_k\left(\frac{p_k}{1-p_k}\right)^{\pi_k}.
\tag{A4.1}
$$

Thus $\pi_k$ is an interpolation weight in log-odds space. The signed terms
$\pi_kz_k$ add to the final logit; they do not form nonnegative shares of the
predicted probability. A large weight on a near-zero logit need not dominate
the prediction. Monotonicity of the sigmoid gives
$\min_k p_k\le p_L\le\max_k p_k$.

Equation (A4.1) is a geometric average of odds, not the arithmetic probability
mixture in (A4.2). L therefore does not generally give $\pi$ a latent-component
probability interpretation for its output distribution. If experts agree,
changing their relative weights cannot change the output.

## P: probability mixture

$$
p_P=\sum_k\pi_kp_k,\qquad
P(Y=y\mid x)=\sum_k\pi_kq_k(y).
\tag{A4.2}
$$

This defines a valid conditional mixture with a latent index $Z$:
$P(Z=k\mid x)=\pi_k$ and $P(Y=y\mid Z=k,x)=q_k(y)$. After observing a label,
Bayes' rule instead gives

$$
r_k(x,y)=P(Z=k\mid x,y)
=\frac{\pi_kq_k(y)}{\sum_j\pi_jq_j(y)}.
\tag{A4.3}
$$

The routing weights are prior mixture probabilities, not posterior
responsibilities or probabilities of being the best expert. The normalized
terms $\pi_kp_k/p_P$ describe the posterior component distribution for $y=1$;
for $y=0$ replace $p_k$ by $1-p_k$. Also
$\min_kp_k\le p_P\le\max_kp_k$.

The training target $t_k\propto q_k(y)^{1/\tau}$ equals (A4.3) at $\tau=1$
under a **uniform expert prior**. It need not equal the posterior under the
learned $\pi$. Its use as supervision does not turn the inference weights into
label-conditioned posteriors.

## F: feature mixture

$$
h_F=\sum_k\pi_kh_k,\qquad
p_F=\sigma(F(h_F)).
\tag{A4.4}
$$

Here $F$ denotes the downstream scalar logit map, including its MLP and final
linear layer. Weights interpolate representations; a nonlinear $F$ prevents
decomposing the prediction into $\pi$-weighted expert logits or probabilities.
There is no general bound by the auxiliary expert probabilities.

If $F(h)=w^\top h+b$ is affine, then
$F(h_F)=\sum_k\pi_k(w^\top h_k+b)$, since $\sum_k\pi_k=1$.
This special case is logit mixing of **shared-head** scores. Those scores need
not equal the separately trained auxiliary expert logits $z_k$.

## Claims that do not follow from any variant

Weights alone do not identify causal modality importance, factual reliability,
calibration, or the benefit of removing an expert. Removing an input or expert
can change the other expert outputs and the router itself. In particular,
$E_{int}$ already consumes both modalities, and the router sees both pooled
embeddings, so $\pi_{img}$ is not the total image contribution to the system.

Even fixed-output decompositions can be nonunique. For P with three experts,
$\sum_k\pi_k=1$ and $\sum_k\pi_kp_k=p_P$ provide at most two independent
linear constraints on three weights. An interior solution generally admits
other weights with the same prediction. L has the same issue with $z_k$.

Report $\pi$ as routing behavior together with expert predictions and held-out
performance. The algebra permits the interpretations above; it does not
establish an empirical gain, prevent collapse, or resolve D1's val-only choice.
