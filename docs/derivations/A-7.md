## 1. Setup and fusion variants

**Claim A7.1 (definitions).** Fix an input $x$, $C\ge2$ classes indexed from
$0$ to $C-1$, $K\ge2$ experts and finite logits $z_k\in\mathbb R^C$.
Let $e_y$ denote the observed label's one-hot vector. Define

$$
p_{kc}=P_k(Y=c\mid x)=\frac{e^{z_{kc}}}{\sum_a e^{z_{ka}}},\quad
\ell_k=\operatorname{CE}(z_k,y)=\operatorname{LSE}_c(z_{kc})-z_{ky},\quad
\pi=\operatorname{softmax}(g).
\tag{A7.1}
$$

Each expert head becomes $z_k=W_k h_k+b_k$ with $C$ output rows.
The feature extractors and $h_k\in\mathbb R^h$ do not depend on $C$.
The router's detached uncertainty inputs become categorical entropies
$\hat H_k=-\sum_c p_{kc}\log p_{kc}/\log C\in[0,1]$.
The bound follows from entropy nonnegativity and
$\operatorname{KL}(p_k\Vert\mathbf1/C)=\log C-H(p_k)\ge0$.

**Claim A7.2 (L).** Logit fusion and its likelihood are

$$
z_L=\sum_k\pi_kz_k,\qquad p_L=\operatorname{softmax}(z_L),\qquad
L_L=-\log p_{L,y}.
\tag{A7.2}
$$

For any two classes $a,b$, $p_{L,a}/p_{L,b}
=\prod_k(p_{ka}/p_{kb})^{\pi_k}$. Equivalently,
$p_{L,c}\propto\prod_k p_{kc}^{\pi_k}$, normalized over classes.
This follows by exponentiating $z_{L,a}-z_{L,b}$; L is a normalized
geometric pool, not generally an arithmetic mixture of expert probabilities.
A per-expert constant logit shift has no effect on the output.

**Claim A7.3 (P: exact categorical mixture).** Define a latent expert $Z$ by
$P(Z=k\mid x)=\pi_k$ and $P(Y=c\mid Z=k,x)=p_{kc}$. Marginalizing gives

$$
p_{P,c}=\sum_k\pi_kp_{kc},\quad \sum_c p_{P,c}=1,\quad
L_P=-\log S,\qquad S=p_{P,y}=\sum_k\pi_kp_{ky}.
\tag{A7.3}
$$

All entries are positive. Thus P is exactly a conditional categorical mixture
of experts, with $\pi$ the prior component probabilities. Bayes' rule gives
the posterior responsibilities $r_k=\pi_kp_{ky}/S$. These are not generally
$\pi_k$, nor probabilities that expert $k$ is the best expert. Componentwise,
$\min_k p_{kc}\le p_{P,c}\le\max_k p_{kc}$.

**Claim A7.4 (F: unchanged feature fusion).** The fusion operation stays
$h_F=\sum_k\pi_kh_k$. Only the task output dimension/link changes:

$$
z_F=W_f\operatorname{MLP}_f(h_F)+b_f\in\mathbb R^C,\quad
p_F=\operatorname{softmax}(z_F),\quad L_F=-\log p_{F,y}.
\tag{A7.4}
$$

A nonlinear downstream head prevents interpreting $\pi$ as shares of the
categorical decision. For an affine head, distributivity gives logit mixing
of shared-head scores; these need not be the auxiliary experts' own logits.
None of L/P/F establishes causal modality importance or calibration.

## 2. Reliability target: P1–P3 for C classes

**Claim A7.5 (target and objective).** For $\tau>0$, with detached losses,

$$
t_k^\tau=\frac{\exp(-\operatorname{sg}(\ell_k)/\tau)}
{\sum_j\exp(-\operatorname{sg}(\ell_j)/\tau)}
=\frac{p_{ky}^{1/\tau}}{\sum_jp_{jy}^{1/\tau}},\qquad
L=L_{L/P/F}+\frac{\lambda_{aux}}K\sum_k\ell_k
+\lambda_{rel}\operatorname{KL}(t^\tau\Vert\pi).
\tag{A7.5}
$$

This identity uses only $\operatorname{CE}(z_k,y)=-\log p_{ky}$, not a binary
factorization. Detachment holds $t$ fixed for backpropagation; it does not
remove its dependence on the training label or on changing expert models.
Without auxiliary CE, experts are not individually trained predictors, so
their losses are not justified as reliability supervision. Auxiliary CE supplies
that individual supervision; it does not prove calibration.

**Claim A7.6 (P1, including ties).** Let $m=\min_k\ell_k$ and
$A=\{k:\ell_k=m\}$. Then

$$
\lim_{\tau\to\infty}t_k^\tau=1/K,\qquad
\lim_{\tau\downarrow0}t_k^\tau=\mathbf1_{k\in A}/|A|.
\tag{A7.6}
$$

**Proof.** At high temperature every exponential tends to one. At low
temperature subtract $m$ from every loss: minimizing terms remain one and
all other terms vanish. The limit is **one-hot only when the minimiser is
unique, and uniform over ties**, including an all-expert tie. These are
label-conditioned target limits, not unconditional inference-router limits.
No step uses $C=2$. $\square$

**Claim A7.7 (P2 and router limits).** At fixed $x$ and fixed experts, let
$\eta_c=P(Y=c\mid x)$ and $\mu=\sum_{c=0}^{C-1}\eta_c t(x,c)$.
For a free simplex vector $\pi$, define
$R(\pi)=\mathbb E_{Y\mid x}\operatorname{KL}(t(x,Y)\Vert\pi)$. Then

$$
R(\pi)-R(\mu)=\operatorname{KL}(\mu\Vert\pi)\ge0,\qquad
\arg\min_\pi R(\pi)=\mu.
\tag{A7.7}
$$

**Proof.** Expanding $R$ leaves a constant minus $\sum_k\mu_k\log\pi_k$;
subtract its value at $\mu$. KL equality requires $\pi=\mu$. At finite
$\tau$, all components are positive; at the boundary use the extended KL
convention and $0\log0=0$. This proof is independent of label cardinality.
Taking limits through the finite $C$-term sum gives uniform $\mu$ at high
temperature and $\sum_c\eta_c\mathbf1_{k\in A(x,c)}/|A(x,c)|$ at low
temperature. A hard optimum requires the same unique winner almost surely
given $x$. A label-dependent winner may yield a soft optimum. $\square$

**Claim A7.8 (dominance qualification, D-011).** For fixed experts and a free
global minimizer $\pi_\lambda$ of $M(\pi)+\lambda R(\pi)$, if
$M\ge M_{\min}$ and $M(\mu)<\infty$, optimality implies

$$
\lambda\operatorname{KL}(\mu\Vert\pi_\lambda)
\le M(\mu)-M(\pi_\lambda)\le M(\mu)-M_{\min}.
\tag{A7.8}
$$

Consequently $\lambda\to\infty$ forces $\pi_\lambda\to\mu$; taking
$\tau\to\infty$ afterward gives static uniform routing. A uniform target
at finite weight does not force a uniform trained router. Parameterized
routers additionally need representability and successful optimization;
finite softmax logits cannot attain a boundary optimum exactly. These are
conditional mathematical limits, not a guarantee for joint training.

**Claim A7.9 (P3: uniform versus router prior).** For any expert prior $a$,
Bayes' rule in the categorical mixture yields

$$
r_k^a=\frac{a_k p_{ky}}{\sum_j a_jp_{jy}}.
\tag{A7.9}
$$

At $\tau=1$, $t=r^a$ for $a_k=1/K$. With $a=\pi$, the posterior instead
contains $\pi_k$. Its odds contain the feedback factor
$r_i^\pi/r_j^\pi=(\pi_i/\pi_j)(p_{iy}/p_{jy})$; uniform-prior targets
remove this factor but do not guarantee balanced routing or prevent collapse.
This is the same mixture identity as A-2, proved here directly for finite
categorical distributions, not a claim that the full objective is EM.
For $\tau\ne1$, the target $t_k^\tau\propto p_{ky}^{1/\tau}$ is
not generally the uniform-prior posterior of the tempered categorical experts
$\tilde p_{kc}=p_{kc}^{1/\tau}/Z_k$, where
$Z_k=\sum_c p_{kc}^{1/\tau}$. That posterior is proportional to
$p_{ky}^{1/\tau}/Z_k$; the expert-dependent $Z_k$ do not generally cancel.
No part of P3 needs $C=2$.

## 3. Router and expert gradients

**Claim A7.10 (L).** Hold expert outputs fixed and use
$\partial\pi_k/\partial g_j=\pi_k(\mathbf1_{k=j}-\pi_j)$ and
$\nabla_{z_L}L_L=p_L-e_y$. The vector chain rule gives

$$
\frac{\partial z_L}{\partial g_j}=\pi_j(z_j-z_L),\qquad
\boxed{\frac{\partial L_L}{\partial g_j}
=\pi_j(p_L-e_y)^\top(z_j-z_L)},\quad
\nabla_{z_j}L_L=\pi_j(p_L-e_y).
\tag{A7.10}
$$

With finite logits the whole router gradient vanishes iff every
$(z_j-z_L)$ is orthogonal to $p_L-e_y$. Unlike the binary scalar result,
this does not require identical predictions. Classwise constant shifts are
trivial orthogonal directions since $\mathbf1^\top(p_L-e_y)=0$; the
condition therefore depends only on logits modulo those shifts.
For example, with $C=3$, $y=0$,
uniform $\pi$ and expert logits $(0,a,-a),(0,-a,a),(0,0,0)$, $a\ne0$,
$z_L=0$ and every inner product is zero despite differing predictions.
This is a constructed counterexample, checked numerically below.

**Claim A7.11 (P).** Differentiating (A7.3) gives
$\partial S/\partial g_j=\pi_j(p_{jy}-S)$ and hence

$$
\boxed{\frac{\partial L_P}{\partial g_j}
=\pi_j-\frac{\pi_jp_{jy}}S=\pi_j-r_j},\qquad
\nabla_{z_j}L_P=r_j(p_j-e_y).
\tag{A7.11}
$$

The expert derivative follows by differentiating $p_{jy}$ through its
softmax. It treats $\pi$ as fixed. The full router gradient is zero iff all
$p_{jy}$ equal $S$; other class probabilities may differ. For example,
$(0.4,0.5,0.1)$ and $(0.4,0.1,0.5)$ agree on the likelihood of $y=0$,
so routing has zero label-loss gradient although predictions differ.
A-5's binary “all probabilities agree” equivalence used that the remaining
probability is determined by $1-p$; it fails for $C>2$.

**Claim A7.12 (detached reliability and combined gradients).** Since $t$ sums
to one, differentiation of $-\sum_k t_k\log\pi_k$ gives

$$
\nabla_g L_{rel}=\pi-t,\qquad
\nabla_g L=\nabla_g L_{L/P/F}+\lambda_{rel}(\pi-t).
\tag{A7.12}
$$

The auxiliary term has no direct $g$ derivative. At fixed $\pi$, its expert
derivative is $(\lambda_{aux}/K)(p_j-e_y)$, while the detached target has
none. Router entropy inputs are detached by default. Shared parameters still
require the complete chain rule, and batch means divide per-sample derivatives
by batch size. Main and reliability gradients can cancel.
For F, writing its downstream logit map as $F(h)$ gives
$\partial L_F/\partial g_j
=\pi_j(p_F-e_y)^\top J_F(h_F)(h_j-h_F)$; no P-style posterior is implied.
A-5's V3 constant-scale absorption and entropy/variance penalty arguments are
unchanged: they concern feature scaling and a Bernoulli gate, not $C$ labels.

**Claim A7.13 (numerical domain).** These derivatives describe the exact,
unclamped likelihood. A stable evaluation of P is

$$
\log S=\operatorname{LSE}_k\{
\operatorname{logsoftmax}(g)_k+
\operatorname{logsoftmax}(z_k)_y\}.
\tag{A7.13}
$$

Independently clipping categorical probabilities can destroy normalization;
a hard clamp on $S$ instead changes the loss and makes its derivative zero
in the clamp's flat region. A7.13 is the multiclass implementation rule
approved in D-018; the existing
binary clamp is retained. At C=2 the exact likelihoods coincide, with the
clamp producing a difference only when active; no empirical frequency of
clamp activation is asserted here.

## 4. Exact binary reduction and the uses of C = 2

**Claim A7.14 (binary reduction).** For $C=2$ set
$a_k=z_{k1}-z_{k0}$. Then $p_{k1}=\sigma(a_k)$ and
$\operatorname{CE}(z_k,y)=\operatorname{BCE}(a_k,y)$ for $y\in\{0,1\}$.
L gives $p_{L,1}=\sigma(\sum_k\pi_k a_k)$; P gives
$p_{P,1}=\sum_k\pi_k\sigma(a_k)$. Writing these scalar quantities as
$a,p$, respectively, reduces (A7.10–11) exactly to

$$
\partial_{g_j}L_L=(\sigma(a)-y)\pi_j(a_j-a),\qquad
\partial_{g_j}L_P=\frac{p-y}{p(1-p)}\pi_j(p_j-p)=\pi_j-r_j.
\tag{A7.14}
$$

The target reduces to A-2's powered Bernoulli likelihood and entropy to §3's
binary entropy. F reduces by taking the difference of its two output logits.
This is exact algebraic equality, including arbitrary common offsets in each
expert's two logits; floating-point checks allow rounding error. Binary
argmax corresponds to a fixed 0.5 threshold, not a val-optimized threshold.

**Claim A7.15 (complete class-count dependency audit of A-2/A-5).** A-2 uses
$C=2$ only to write $q_k=p_k^y(1-p_k)^{1-y}$, the two-term label expectation,
and its specific Bernoulli examples/tempered normalizers. Replace those by
$p_{ky}$, a $C$-term expectation and $\sum_c p_{kc}^{1/\tau}$.
A-5 uses $C=2$ for sigmoid/BCE derivatives, the scalar probability expression
$(p-y)/(p(1-p))$, the two-sided binary clamp and the zero-gradient equivalences.
The softmax **over experts**, Bayes identity, KL risk-gap proof, temperature
limits and detached-target derivative never require $C=2$. The V3 penalty
uses a two-way modality gate even for a multiclass task; it is not a
restriction to two label classes.

## 5. D-018 approved evaluation and pre-extension MATH_SPEC audit

**Claim A7.16 (approved metrics and checked provenance).** MVSA-Single uses
$C=3$ and Food-101 uses $C=101$ per D-012. Use accuracy as primary and macro-F1
as secondary. QMF reports accuracy on these benchmarks (Zhang et al., ICML
2023, PMLR 202:41753–41769, §5.1–5.2 and Table 5, PDF p. 16:
[paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/zhang23ar/zhang23ar.pdf)).
PDF also reports accuracy on them (Cao et al., ICML 2024, PMLR
235:5608–5628, Table 9, PDF p. 20; averages also in Table 1, p. 6:
[published paper](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlresearch/v235/main/assets/cao24c/cao24c.pdf)).
These are the clean-condition ($\epsilon=0$) tables used in
[literature_numbers.csv](../literature_numbers.csv); no result values are
repeated here.
Predict $\hat y=\arg\max_c p_c$, with the smallest class index resolving
exact ties. No threshold is fitted. Macro-F1 averages the $C$ classwise
$2TP/(2TP+FP+FN)$ scores; use zero when that denominator is zero.
No dataset is accessed by this derivation.

**Claim A7.17 (approved significance rules).** For each paired seed, let
$a_i=\mathbf1[\hat y_i^A=y_i]$, $b_i=\mathbf1[\hat y_i^B=y_i]$.
Accuracy difference is $N^{-1}\sum_i(a_i-b_i)$. Use §10's 10,000 paired
bootstrap draws of sample indices, the same draw for both predictors, a
percentile 95% interval and its stated two-sided tail convention capped at
one. McNemar operates on the two discordant correctness counts, regardless
of the number of label classes: exact binomial below 25 discordant pairs,
otherwise continuity-corrected chi-square; zero discordance gives $p=1$.
DeLong as specified tests binary AUROC and is not used here. Across-seed
paired t-tests in §10 use accuracy differences for these two
datasets, with the existing seed counts; samples and seeds are not pooled
as independent observations. D-018 adds both datasets to the headline Holm
family. Bootstrap intervals/tests require the same sampling assumptions
as §10 and do not establish an empirical gain by themselves.
