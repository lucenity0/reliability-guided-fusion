## 1. Router gradients

For one sample let $\pi=\operatorname{softmax}(g)$, finite expert logits $z_k$,
$p_k=\sigma(z_k)$, and binary $y$. Derivatives below are with respect to the
router logits with expert outputs held fixed:

$$
\frac{\partial\pi_k}{\partial g_j}
=\pi_k(\mathbf{1}_{k=j}-\pi_j).
\tag{A5.1}
$$

### Variant L

With $z=\sum_k\pi_kz_k$ and $L_L=\log(1+e^z)-yz$,

$$
\frac{\partial z}{\partial g_j}=\pi_j(z_j-z),\qquad
\boxed{\frac{\partial L_L}{\partial g_j}
=(\sigma(z)-y)\pi_j(z_j-z).}
\tag{A5.2}
$$

Proof: substitute (A5.1) into the weighted sum, then use
$\partial L_L/\partial z=\sigma(z)-y$. For binary labels and finite logits,
the scalar factor is nonzero and every $\pi_j>0$. Thus the **whole vector**
vanishes exactly when all experts agree. An individual component can vanish
when $z_j=z$ even if other experts disagree. Small softmax weights, confident
correct predictions, finite-precision saturation and minibatch cancellation
can still make the practical gradient small.

### Variant P

Let $p=\sum_k\pi_kp_k$ and $L_P=-y\log p-(1-y)\log(1-p)$. Then

$$
\frac{\partial p}{\partial g_j}=\pi_j(p_j-p),\qquad
\boxed{\frac{\partial L_P}{\partial g_j}
=\frac{p-y}{p(1-p)}\pi_j(p_j-p).}
\tag{A5.3}
$$

Equivalently, $q_j=p_j^y(1-p_j)^{1-y}$, $S=\sum_k\pi_kq_k$, and
$r_j=\pi_jq_j/S$ yield

$$
L_P=-\log S,\qquad
\frac{\partial L_P}{\partial g_j}=\pi_j-r_j.
\tag{A5.4}
$$

This follows from $\partial S/\partial g_j=\pi_j(q_j-S)$.
The whole gradient vanishes exactly when all $p_j$ agree, under the same
finite-logit assumptions. Equation (A5.4) uses the router-prior posterior $r$,
not the uniform-prior reliability target $t$.

MATH_SPEC §6 clamps $p$ to $[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]$. Equations (A5.3–4)
describe the unclamped loss and the interior of that interval. A hard clamp
has zero derivative outside it; at the boundaries the implementation chooses
a derivative convention. Tests avoid the kink and separately verify the
constant-loss region. This qualification matters when debugging saturation.

### Detached reliability target and total routing signal

Hold $t=\operatorname{softmax}(-\operatorname{sg}(\ell)/\tau)$ fixed during
backpropagation. Since $\sum_k t_k=1$,

$$
L_{rel}=\sum_k t_k\log t_k-\sum_k t_k\log\pi_k,\qquad
\boxed{\frac{\partial L_{rel}}{\partial g_j}=\pi_j-t_j.}
\tag{A5.5}
$$

Differentiate $\log\pi_k=g_k-\log\sum_l e^{g_l}$ to obtain (A5.5).
The auxiliary expert loss has no direct $g$ derivative with expert outputs
fixed, so $\partial L/\partial g_j$ is (A5.2) or (A5.3), plus
$\lambda_{rel}(\pi_j-t_j)$. Mean batch losses divide each sample's derivative
by batch size. Gradients for shared parameters still require the full chain
rule. Main and reliability terms can cancel; no collapse-prevention or
performance guarantee follows from a nonzero individual term.

## 2. Exact constant-gate result for V3

Verified against frozen V3
[model.py, lines 200–214 and 227–261](https://github.com/lucenity0/A-Unified-Adaptive-Framework-for-Multimodal-Data-Fusion-with-Dynamic-Modality-Reweighting/blob/75c6b9f5424cfd65b04a3c29c89ded40a7ce38cd/src/model.py#L200).
The classifier receives $[\alpha v;(1-\alpha)u;\alpha]$ after cross-modal
processing and normalization; $v,u$ are therefore branch features, not
necessarily unmixed modalities. Write its first affine layer as

$$
a=\alpha W_vv+(1-\alpha)W_uu+w_\alpha\alpha+b.
\tag{A5.6}
$$

**Proposition (functional non-identifiability for constant gates).** For any
constant gates $c,d\in(0,1)$, a classifier using $\alpha=c$ has the same
input-output function as one using $\alpha=d$ under

$$
W'_v=\frac{c}{d}W_v,\quad
W'_u=\frac{1-c}{1-d}W_u,\quad
w'_\alpha=w_\alpha,\quad b'=b+(c-d)w_\alpha.
\tag{A5.7}
$$

**Proof.** Substitute (A5.7) into (A5.6) with $\alpha=d$. Every term equals
the original preactivation with $\alpha=c$. All subsequent deterministic
layers therefore agree; with dropout they agree for the same masks (and in
distribution). This is equality of predictions, not of regularized training
objectives: weight decay and gate penalties need not be invariant. Endpoints
$0,1$ are excluded because one branch disappears. $\square$

For a sample-dependent gate, absorbing a change $\alpha(x)\mapsto d(x)$ would
generally require sample-dependent ratios $\alpha(x)/d(x)$ and
$(1-\alpha(x))/(1-d(x))$ in shared weight matrices, plus a sample-dependent
bias correction. There is no general shared-affine reparameterization of
this form. The explicit $\alpha$ input can help a nonlinear MLP compensate:
on an interior range it has information to recover $v=(\alpha v)/\alpha$
and $u=((1-\alpha)u)/(1-\alpha)$. That observation is not an exact finite-MLP
absorption theorem and does not imply that training finds such a function.

## 3. Entropy pressure and its competing variance term

Frozen V3 [regularization.py, lines 22–36](https://github.com/lucenity0/A-Unified-Adaptive-Framework-for-Multimodal-Data-Fusion-with-Dynamic-Modality-Reweighting/blob/75c6b9f5424cfd65b04a3c29c89ded40a7ce38cd/src/regularization.py#L22)
implements mean negative Bernoulli entropy and negative `torch.var(alpha)`.
For $B>1$, use the sample variance with denominator $B-1$, matching that call.
In the unclamped interior define

$$
R=\frac{\lambda_H}{B}\sum_i
\{\alpha_i\log\alpha_i+(1-\alpha_i)\log(1-\alpha_i)\}
-\frac{\lambda_D}{B-1}\sum_i(\alpha_i-\bar\alpha)^2,
\quad \bar\alpha=\frac1B\sum_i\alpha_i.
\tag{A5.8}
$$

For nonnegative coefficients,

$$
\frac{\partial R}{\partial\alpha_i}
=\frac{\lambda_H}{B}\log\frac{\alpha_i}{1-\alpha_i}
-\frac{2\lambda_D}{B-1}(\alpha_i-\bar\alpha).
\tag{A5.9}
$$

The entropy term alone has its unique minimum at $1/2$, as its second
derivative is $1/[\alpha(1-\alpha)]>0$. The variance term moves samples away
from their batch mean. If $\alpha_i=\sigma(s_i)$, multiply (A5.9) by
$\alpha_i(1-\alpha_i)$ to obtain the gate-logit derivative; saturation can
weaken both signals. V3 clamps entropy inputs, so its entropy derivative is
zero outside that clamp's interior. The analysis below is around $1/2$,
where the clamp is inactive.

Let $P=I-\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top/B$. At $\alpha=\tfrac12\mathbf{1}$,

$$
\nabla^2 R=\frac{4\lambda_H}{B}I-\frac{2\lambda_D}{B-1}P.
\tag{A5.10}
$$

The common-shift eigenvalue is $4\lambda_H/B$; all centered-spread directions
have eigenvalue $4\lambda_H/B-2\lambda_D/(B-1)$. With $\lambda_H>0$, the
all-half gate is a strict local minimum of these two penalties when

$$
\lambda_D < 2\lambda_H\frac{B-1}{B}.
\tag{A5.11}
$$

Above that threshold, the variance term makes centered perturbations decrease
the penalty locally. At equality the quadratic spread term is zero and
higher-order terms matter. These statements concern the penalty alone;
the main loss, other regularizers, optimizer, gate parameterization and batch
composition can alter the full training behavior. Batch size one does not
admit the sample-variance formula.

Constant-gate non-identifiability and entropy pressure are **mechanisms
consistent with** collapse. They do not prove what caused a particular V3
run, nor establish that V4 must avoid collapse. No empirical collapse numbers
are repeated here without a run artifact establishing them. The L/P formulas
instead show explicitly how disagreements between expert predictions produce
a direct routing signal under the stated conditions.
