## Verified comparisons

**Mixtures of experts.** Jacobs et al. (1991), p. 81 Figure 1 and p. 82
equations (1.3–1.5), already combine an input-dependent gate with expert
likelihoods and responsibility-weighted learning [1]. Jordan and Jacobs
(1994), §2.3 pp. 188–189 and §2.5 pp. 190–191, distinguish prior routing
probabilities from posterior responsibilities and develop EM training [2].
V4 adopts that mixture interpretation only for Variant P; its uniform-prior
tempered target omits the current router prior. The supervised KL target and
its unit-temperature posterior identity should not be presented as a new
general mixture principle or as EM for V4's entire objective.

**Learning to defer and selective prediction.** Mozannar and Sontag (2020),
§3 equations (1–2) and §4, PDF pp. 2–3, study a predictor/rejector system that
can choose an expert's decision, with an expert-dependent system cost and
a consistent surrogate [3]. Its §3 also distinguishes constant-cost rejection
from expert-dependent deferral. V4 shares the idea of allocating decisions
using relative performance. It ordinarily blends learned expert outputs
rather than deferring to a downstream decision maker; it does not inherit
that surrogate's consistency theorem. Small-temperature training targets
are not themselves a hard inference deferral policy (A-2).

**QMF.** Zhang et al. (2023), §3.2 Theorem 2 and §4 equations (9–18), PDF
pp. 4–6, connect dynamic unimodal prediction weights to modality losses and
use energy-based uncertainty with a loss-history regularizer [4]. V4's
proposed difference is an explicitly multimodal interaction expert inside
the routed prediction set, supervised jointly with the unimodal experts.
Using losses to teach reliability is therefore not new by itself. QMF's
bound assumptions do not automatically apply to V4's learned experts, KL
objective or data. A smaller error upper bound is not by itself an observed
performance advantage on these benchmarks.

**PDF.** Cao et al. (2024), §3.3 equations (4–6), §4 equations (7–10) and
Appendix C.6, predict true-class confidence, combine modality-specific and
cross-modality confidence into Co-Belief, and calibrate it relatively [5].
This is prior work on learning reliability from label-dependent signals;
it cannot be described as merely unsupervised confidence weighting. V4
instead trains a simplex router toward a normalized tempered loss target
over image, text and interaction experts. PDF's Appendix E explicitly
qualifies relative calibration as empirical; V4 should not imply it inherits
a blanket reliability guarantee from this comparison.

The differences above are an interpretation of the cited mechanisms, not a
claim that these papers exclude every possible extension to interaction
experts. A claim to be the first such architecture would require a broader
literature search than these four comparisons.

## What can be claimed, and what must be tested

The specified V4 design combines frozen multimodal features, an interaction
expert, unimodal experts and a reliability-supervised router. The defensible
research question is whether routing over that set improves meme prediction
relative to static fusion of the same experts and dynamic unimodal-only
alternatives. The comparison must hold features, splits, seeds and relevant
training choices fixed, as required by the repository protocol.

Neither the KL objective, softmax temperature, conditional expert selection
nor the posterior identity establishes novelty alone. A-2 supplies a precise
description of this target family and its limits, not a performance theorem.
The label-conditioned target is used for training only; evaluation must
derive router weights from input features and model outputs, never test labels.

The planned static/unimodal controls, drop-interaction ablation, and
reliability-loss ablation address different questions: the value of dynamic
routing, the additional interaction expert, and the target supervision.
Results must separate these effects. Negative comparisons would narrow the
contribution rather than justify stronger claims. No experiment is performed
or performance number borrowed in this derivation.

**Proposed contribution sentence — [OWNER review required]:**

> We study reliability-supervised routing among unimodal and interaction
> experts for multimodal meme classification. The target has explicit
> temperature limits and a classical uniform-prior posterior interpretation;
> the empirical question is whether this expert set and supervision improve
> over matched static and unimodal dynamic-fusion controls.
