## Assumptions and notation

Fix an input $x$, finite expert logits $z_k(x)$ and $K\ge2$. Experts remain
fixed throughout the population optimization. Let $p_k=\sigma(z_k)\in(0,1)$,
$y\in\{0,1\}$, $q_k(y\mid x)=p_k^y(1-p_k)^{1-y}$, and
$\ell_k(x,y)=-\log q_k(y\mid x)$. For $\tau>0$,

$$
t_k^\tau(x,y)=\frac{e^{-\ell_k(x,y)/\tau}}
{\sum_j e^{-\ell_j(x,y)/\tau}}
=\frac{q_k(y\mid x)^{1/\tau}}{\sum_jq_j(y\mid x)^{1/\tau}}.
\tag{A2.1}
$$

All finite-temperature components are positive. Detachment means that the
target is held fixed when differentiating the reliability loss; it does not
make the target independent of training labels or of evolving expert models.

## P1: limits of the target

**Proposition.** At fixed $(x,y)$ let
$m=\min_j\ell_j$, $S=\{k:\ell_k=m\}$. Then

$$
\lim_{\tau\to\infty}t_k^\tau=\frac1K,\qquad
\lim_{\tau\downarrow0}t_k^\tau=
\frac{\mathbf{1}_{k\in S}}{|S|}.
\tag{A2.2}
$$

**Proof.** For the first limit, each exponential in (A2.1) tends to one.
For the second, subtract $m$ before exponentiation:

$$
t_k^\tau=\frac{e^{-(\ell_k-m)/\tau}}
{|S|+\sum_{j\notin S}e^{-(\ell_j-m)/\tau}}.
\tag{A2.3}
$$

Every non-minimizing term vanishes while each minimizing term stays one.
This also covers all experts tying. The limit is one-hot **if and only if**
the minimizer is unique. $\square$

This is a label-conditioned supervision limit. It is not yet a statement
about the inference router, which sees $x$ but not $y$.

## P2: the population minimizer

Define $\mu^\tau(x)=\mathbb{E}_{Y\mid x}[t^\tau(x,Y)]$ and optimize a free
$\pi\in\Delta^{K-1}$, independent of the realized label, at this fixed $x$.

**Proposition.** The unique minimizer of the expected reliability loss is

$$
\boxed{\pi^\star(x)=\mu^\tau(x)
=\mathbb{E}_{Y\mid x}[t^\tau(x,Y)].}
\tag{A2.4}
$$

**Proof.** Expanding KL and collecting the terms that depend on $\pi$ gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal R_\tau(\pi)
&=\mathbb E\!\left[\sum_k t_k^\tau\log t_k^\tau\mid x\right]
-\sum_k\mu_k^\tau\log\pi_k,\\
\mathcal R_\tau(\pi)-\mathcal R_\tau(\mu^\tau)
&=\mathrm{KL}(\mu^\tau\Vert\pi)\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{A2.5}
$$

Equality holds only at $\pi=\mu^\tau$. At finite $\tau$, every $\mu_k^\tau>0$,
so any zero component of $\pi$ gives infinite risk. With zero limiting
components, use $0\log0=0$ and the extended KL convention; equality still
requires identical distributions. $\square$

This is a pointwise, unrestricted-simplex result. A parameterized router must
be able to represent these optima simultaneously for all inputs, and its
optimizer must attain them, before the same conclusion applies to that
router. A finite-logit softmax cannot attain boundary solutions exactly,
although it can approach them. Jointly changing experts and optimizing the
main loss are additional issues outside this fixed-expert proposition.

### What P1 implies for this reliability-only optimum

Since $0\le t_k^\tau\le1$, bounded convergence (or simply the finite binary
label sum) permits the expectation and limit to interchange:

$$
\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\mu^\tau=\frac{\mathbf1}{K},\qquad
\lim_{\tau\downarrow0}\mu_k^\tau
=\mathbb E_{Y\mid x}\!\left[
\frac{\mathbf1_{k\in S(x,Y)}}{|S(x,Y)|}\right].
\tag{A2.6}
$$

If the minimum is unique almost surely, the second limit is the conditional
distribution of the winning expert. It is one-hot precisely when the same
unique winner occurs almost surely given $x$. Deterministic labels given $x$
are sufficient **only with a unique winner**. Ties remain soft.

**Synthetic counterexample to unconditional hard routing.** Let two experts
predict $p_1=0.9$, $p_2=0.1$ and $P(Y=1\mid x)=1/2$. Expert 1 uniquely wins
when $Y=1$ and expert 2 when $Y=0$. Both targets become hard as $\tau\downarrow0$,
but their population-optimal router remains $(1/2,1/2)$. These values are a
constructed example, not dataset results.

### Adding the main loss

For fixed experts write $J_\lambda(\pi)=M(\pi)+\lambda\mathcal R_\tau(\pi)$,
where $M$ is the expected main loss. At finite positive $\lambda$, its
minimizer need not be $\mu^\tau$; a uniform target alone is insufficient.
For a freely representable global minimizer $\pi_\lambda$, if $M$ is bounded
below by $M_{\min}$ and $M(\mu^\tau)<\infty$, comparison with $\mu^\tau$ yields

$$
\lambda\,\mathrm{KL}(\mu^\tau\Vert\pi_\lambda)
\le M(\mu^\tau)-M(\pi_\lambda)
\le M(\mu^\tau)-M_{\min}.
\tag{A2.7}
$$

Thus reliability dominance ($\lambda\to\infty$ at fixed experts and $\tau$)
forces $\pi_\lambda\to\mu^\tau$. Taking the high-temperature limit afterward
gives uniform routing. This sufficient sequential-limit argument is not a
claim that ordinary finite-weight joint training has the same endpoint.
A main loss constant in $\pi$ is another sufficient case; in the actual
training problem, vanishing main-loss routing gradients must be established,
not assumed from the temperature alone.

For example, in Variant P with deterministic $y=1$ and expert probabilities
$(0.9,0.1)$, the derivative of $-\log(0.1+0.8\pi_1)$ at $\pi_1=1/2$ is
$-1.6$. The uniform-target KL derivative is zero there. Uniform routing is
therefore not stationary for their sum at any finite reliability weight.

## P3: posterior responsibilities and the uniform prior

Variant P defines $P(Y=y\mid x)=\sum_k a_k(x)q_k(y\mid x)$ for an expert
prior $a\in\Delta^{K-1}$. Introduce a latent expert index $Z$. Bayes' rule gives

$$
r_k^a(x,y)=P(Z=k\mid x,y)
=\frac{a_k(x)q_k(y\mid x)}{\sum_j a_j(x)q_j(y\mid x)}.
\tag{A2.8}
$$

At $a_k=1/K$ and $\tau=1$, (A2.8) is exactly (A2.1). For the learned
router prior $a=\pi$, the posterior instead includes the factor $\pi_k$.
This matches the classical distinction between input-conditioned priors and
label-conditioned responsibilities: see Jordan and Jacobs (1994), §2.3,
pp. 188–189, equations (2.10–2.12), and the EM construction in §2.5,
pp. 190–191 ([author-hosted paper](https://www2.bcs.rochester.edu/sites/robbie/jordanjacobs.nc94.pdf)).

For $\tau\ne1$, (A2.1) uses powered likelihoods. It is not the posterior of
the original Bernoulli mixture. Normalizing each powered Bernoulli over
$y$ would introduce expert-dependent normalizers, so that alternate model
does not generally give (A2.1) with a uniform prior either.

Classical EM computes responsibilities under the current model and uses
them in an expected complete-data objective. V4's detached uniform-prior
target plus main and auxiliary losses is not that EM algorithm, nor does
this identity supply EM's likelihood-improvement guarantee. The identity
is specific to P's probability mixture; L and F do not generally have the
same mixture likelihood.

### Uniform prior and feedback

The odds of two router-prior responsibilities satisfy
$r_i^\pi/r_j^\pi=(\pi_i/\pi_j)(q_i/q_j)$; the target odds are
$t_i^\tau/t_j^\tau=(q_i/q_j)^{1/\tau}$. A larger current routing weight
therefore directly boosts its own responsibility in the former, all else
fixed. Uniform-prior targets remove this particular feedback factor. They
do not remove learning feedback through the experts, guarantee balanced
weights, or prevent collapse. If one expert is consistently superior, a
concentrated router may be a valid optimum.

## Proposed paper language — [OWNER review required]

> The reliability target interpolates between uniform weights and a uniform
> distribution over the best experts for a labeled example. Its population
> optimum is the conditional expectation of that target. The low-temperature
> optimum is hard routing only when a unique winning expert is determined by
> the input; static uniform routing at high temperature requires the stated
> reliability-only or dominance assumptions.

> At unit temperature, the target equals posterior responsibility in a
> probability mixture with a uniform expert prior. This is an adaptation of
> a classical mixture identity, not a new EM principle or a guarantee for the
> jointly trained router.
